### 2. `docs/stability.md` — the four surfaces and what 1.0 means (S)

**Demand.** Nothing defines 1.0: SECURITY.md:48 says "latest release only, while the project is pre-1.0"; ROADMAP:65 defers Quicklisp to 1.0. ### 3. CCL pin semantics: a borrowed buffer must not stop the world (M)

**Demand.** BOUNDARY §4 and `docs/benchmarks.md:60` ("the inbound side is a true zero-copy borrow") were measured on SBCL. On CCL, CFFI's `with-pointer-to-vector-data` is `ccl:with-pointer-to-ivector` (`cffi-openmcl.lisp:109`), whose docstring says "The GC is disabled during execution of BODY" and whose body runs under `without-gcing` (`~/ccl/lib/macros.lisp:3355-3367`). `lisp/src/ffi.lisp:173` (`call-with-bytes-arg`, hence every `:string` via `call-with-utf8-arg` at :184) and :205 (`call-with-vec-arg`) use it — so every `:string`/`:bytes`/`:vec` argument suspends GC process-wide for the whole export, callbacks included (rx's `for_each_match(text, f)` conses per match inside it). Two judges reproduced it independently in standalone CCL 1.13 scripts (0 collections across hundreds of MB consed inside a pin; a concurrent allocator stalled). A real host-specific hazard, invisible to §12 because the row is "runtime-check", and documented nowhere.
**Scope.** On CCL, `cffi:foreign-alloc` + memcpy under `unwind-protect` (pin only for the copy; not `with-foreign-object`, which is `%stack-block` on CCL, `cffi-openmcl.lisp:64`); ~15 lines in `ffi.lisp` behind `#+ccl`. Qualify BOUNDARY §4 and benchmarks.md per host. ECL's `si:make-foreign-data-from-array` (`cffi-ecl.lisp:142`) aliases storage without inhibiting GC — document, no change.
**Acceptance.** `tests/suite/v05.lisp`: `v05.pin-does-not-stop-the-world` — a long pinned call (rx `count` over a multi-MiB haystack, or wordbag `for_each_word` with a consing callback) while a second thread conses; assert the thread progresses and the host GC counter advances during the call; non-vacuous on all five hosts (m7's rule: named skip with cause, never silent green). Bench refresh lands in item 11.

### 4. Make the fuzzers falsifiable (S)

**Demand.** `m4h.thread-race` (`tests/suite/m4.lisp:18-53`, seed `(+ 1000 tid)` at :31) and `m4h.random-op-sequence` (:84-118, `(make-lcg 42)` at :89) assert only "no unexpected condition". `test_live_word_bags`/`test_live_allocations` (`examples/wordbag/src/lib.rs:260-266`) exist and are used by `m3.string-allocations` and `m4.gc-finalization` (`m1.lisp:110-149`) but not by the fuzzers — a leaked in-flight count or a free that never reached Rust passes today. §12's §4 row records "no concurrent-reload test" for gen-N dealloc.

**Acceptance.** Both fuzzers end with `(is (<= (live-bags) (+ before slack)))` and `(is (= before (live-allocs)))`; new `m4h.reload-under-load`; the §12 note is replaced by the test name; a `workflow_dispatch` input sets the seed.

### 5. Manifest skew rule + informational `:rulisp-version` (S)

**Demand.** `git show v0.3.0:lisp/src/manifest.lisp` has zero `on-dump`; `meta.rs:256` emits `:schema 1` unconditionally and `:on-dump` at :268-269. A 0.4-built on_dump crate on a 0.3 loader loads cleanly with its hook silently dropped — the §10 hazard returning through loader skew, which becomes routine at Quicklisp cadence. There is no rule for *keys* a crate's correctness relies on (DESIGN §11 item 8 got it right for *types*).
**Decision (the judges split; this resolves it).** Do **not** bump `:schema` to 2 for on_dump: the 0.4.0 loader (`manifest.lisp:127`, `(<= schema 1)`) supports `:on-dump` fully and is the population when 0.5 ships; bumping would refuse every 0.5 on_dump crate there to guard 0.3 loaders that cannot be fixed retroactively. Instead: BOUNDARY §11 gains the rule "a key the crate's correctness relies on raises `:schema` in the release that introduces it; enhancement keys (`:doc`, `:rulisp-version`) do not", with a §12 row; the macro emits `(:rulisp-version "<rulisp-runtime CARGO_PKG_VERSION>")`; the loader parses it and signals a `style-warning` when the crate's major.minor is newer than the loader's; CHANGELOG states the 0.3-loader exposure plainly.
**Acceptance.** `tests/golden/wordbag.manifest.sexp` and the `tests/m1-handwritten` oracle co-updated in one commit, `manifest_matches_golden` byte-identical; `fx.unknown-keys-ignored` still green; new `v05.newer-rulisp-warns` (fixture `:rulisp-version "99.0.0"` loads with a warning, no error); describe (item 6) prints it.

### 9. `:string` ASCII fast path — verdict on candidate (d) (S)

**Demand.** benchmarks.md:67-68 ("64 KiB costs ~1 ms where the same bytes as `:bytes` cost 40 µs") is a measurement, not a demand, and ":bytes and decode what you need" stays right for octet payloads — but every rx method takes `&str`, so for the quickstart crate "pass :bytes" only relocates the `babel` call into user code. Both judges reproduced, standalone on the baseline SBCL 2.1.11 at 64 KiB ASCII: babel ~336/546 µs encode/decode, a typed check-and-store loop ~132/195 µs, memcpy floor ~24 µs — and `sb-ext:octets-to-string` at ~2.4 ms, so "use the native codec" is refuted.
**Scope.** In `ffi.lisp` `call-with-utf8-arg` (:181) and `foreign-utf8` (:149): typed ASCII loop first, babel on the first char/byte ≥ 128; host-neutral; no wire change; the copy contract (BOUNDARY §4, :67) untouched, so zero-copy `:string` is *not* reopened.
**Acceptance.** `tests/bench.lisp` gains "string round trip (64 KiB ASCII)" (no such row exists today — only 1 KiB non-ASCII at :68) and an rx "count over 1 MiB" row; ≤ 450 µs on the baseline host; `v05.utf8-fastpath-boundary` identity tests: empty, all-ASCII, non-ASCII first/last char, Latin-1-range chars (code 128–255), 4-byte chars.

## Explicitly not in v0.5

- **A flagship example** (sqlite/duckdb, tokenizer, image codec, `examples/proc`). Binding v0.4 refusal (`v04-plan.md:95`: "build the v0.5 flagship from what they actually request") with unchanged cause: the tracker holds one closed soundness issue and no request; the sqlite proposal chose "by demand generated, not by request", which is the speculative case by definition, and its L effort is the whole v0.3 cycle again. Reopen trigger: an external issue naming the crate — that is the 0.6 flagship.
- **Vocabulary round 2** — `(:vec :string)`, multiple values, tagged enums → conditions, records by value. Refused-with-cause in `v03-async-plan.md:1557-1573` and v0.4; no new demand case in the tree or tracker. Kept from the consumer proposal: the wire's `last_error` carries only (type, msg), so a core enum mapping yields a class with no slots, while the fetch veneer pattern gives slots and restarts.
- **Zero-copy `:string`** (SBCL `simple-base-string` pin) — refused v0.3 §7/v0.4; item 9 takes the codec gain under the unchanged copy contract. **Host-native codec** (`sb-ext:octets-to-string`) — refuted by measurement.
- **Retroactive `:schema 2` for on_dump** — over-refuses 0.4.0 loaders (item 5).
- **Display-driven print-object; macro error-span polish** — v0.4 refusal holds; a veneer can specialize `print-object` today; item 6 answers the observed need.
- **10-minute soak workflow** — never required, RSS/fd bounds flake on shared runners, hypotheses anticipatory; reconsider after item 4 is green.
- **cargo-fuzz on the manifest parser, ASan/TSan, a separate handle-state-machine model** — the only parser is the Lisp reader under `*read-eval*` nil over a closed grammar reading an artifact SECURITY.md classes as trusted code; sanitizers cannot coexist with SBCL's signal-driven GC; `handle.lisp` is 112 lines already driven by item 4's fuzzers. Miri (item 10) is the sanitizer that fits.
- **`cargo rulisp new`, template crate, offline lint** — no request; the quickstart scaffold is six Cargo.toml lines and `prepare-bindings` (`crate.lisp:248`) is the lint.
- **LispWorks/Allegro/ABCL/ocicl; macOS x86_64 blobs** — zero consumer stories; a blob without a test host repeats the mistake v0.4 fixed.
- **Quicklisp submission** — user decision at 1.0; prerequisites listed in item 2 only.
- **Deprecation machinery, CI benchmark gating, blanket `From<E>`/anyhow, constructor `&key`, push/doorbell callbacks, on_dump reload semantics, dlclose, any ABI bump** — unchanged refusals; no new demand case.
